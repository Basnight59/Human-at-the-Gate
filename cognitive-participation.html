Human at the Gate / Part 05 of 07

Cognitive participation

Automation can increase output while reducing human understanding.

Research on “cognitive offloading” finds that when people hand a task entirely to a tool, they tend to remember and understand less of it. The Sphinx Method deliberately keeps the human inside every step, because learning is itself one of the outputs.

Several systems at once

The method engages several systems at once:

  • Reading
  • Listening
  • Speaking
  • Recalling
  • Comparing
  • Judging
  • Reformulating

Effortful, active and multisensory engagement is well established in general neuroscience as supporting learning and lasting change in the brain.

A hypothesis, not a proven result

Human-centered platform orchestration may function as a neuroplastic learning environment, because it repeatedly engages sensory, linguistic, analytical, memory and decision-making systems rather than outsourcing them.

The claim is testable, and we invite study of it.
